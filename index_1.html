Doctoral research interest

My proposed work sits between two literatures that rarely speak to each other.

The first is the process tradition in identity development — Luyckx's five-dimensional model and Crocetti's extension of it, which treat identity not as a fixed outcome but as repeating cycles of exploration and commitment. The second is the dimensional turn in personality pathology, particularly Criterion A of the DSM-5 Alternative Model, where identity appears as a component of personality functioning itself.

The question I want to answer is whether the association between identity processes and features of personality pathology holds in culturally plural, non-Western samples — and whether individualism–collectivism moderates it. Nearly all instrument development in this area has happened in Western settings, which makes cross-cultural measurement invariance a live methodological problem rather than a footnote.

Research

Café Spaces as Emotional Anchors

  • MSc dissertation, UEL
  • 2025
  • Supervisor: Dr Sonya Dineva
  • 14 interviews · 7/7 design

Semi-structured interviews with café-based and home-based workers in Mumbai, analysed with Braun & Clarke reflexive thematic analysis, examining how physical environment shapes motivation, productivity and emotional regulation. The balanced design allowed direct comparison across the two working contexts.

Gratitude and Self-Esteem in Young Adults

  • Independent research, NMIMS
  • 2024
  • Supervisor: Dr Heena Kamble
  • n = 98

Quantitative study administering the GQ-6 and the Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale, analysed correlationally. Identified a significant positive association between dispositional gratitude and self-esteem.

Hesitation Towards Therapy in India

  • Research methods, NMIMS
  • 2022
  • Supervisor: Dr Aditi Dey Sarkar
  • n = 114 · mixed methods

Survey across Mumbai, Ahmedabad and Gujarat combined with structured interviews of licensed practitioners, examining cultural, social and structural barriers to help-seeking. Shame, family expectation, religious belief and cost recurred across both strands — the study that first pointed me towards collectivist cultural values as an explanatory variable rather than background.
